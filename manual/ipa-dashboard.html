Country Dashboard Guide

Your country dashboard is your workspace on the IPAWAS platform. Everything you do here — managing your investment profile, publishing opportunities, responding to investors, and building your team — represents your country to the world. This guide walks you through every section in plain, practical terms.

You can only see your own country's data

The platform automatically restricts your access to your own member state. You cannot view or edit another country's content, and they cannot see yours.

1. Your Dashboard Overview

When you log in as IPA Staff, you land on your country's overview page at /dashboard/your-country/. This is your starting point every day.

2. Managing Your Country Profile

Your country profile is what investors see when they visit your page on the public IPAWAS website. A complete, well-written profile builds credibility and attracts serious investors. Navigate to Sidebar → Profile.

Image quality matters

Use high-resolution images. Blurry or pixelated images look unprofessional to international investors. The hero image is the first thing they see — make it striking.

Fill in or update each field

3

B. Economic Data

Go to Profile → Economic Data. This provides investors with key economic indicators about your country.

Fill in your country's:

  • GDP, GDP growth rate, inflation rate
  • Population and major cities
  • Currency and exchange rate
  • Key industries and exports
  • Ease of doing business ranking

Keep this data up to date — investors use it to make decisions. Aim to review it at least once a year.

C. Media Gallery

Go to Profile → Media Gallery. Upload photos and videos that showcase your country's investment environment.

Good content to include in your gallery:

  • Infrastructure (ports, airports, industrial zones)
  • Business districts and special economic zones
  • Agricultural land and natural resources
  • Manufacturing facilities
  • Video testimonials from existing investors

D. SEO Settings

Go to Profile → SEO Settings. SEO (Search Engine Optimisation) determines how your country profile appears when someone searches for it on Google.

  • Meta Title — The title shown in Google search results (keep it under 60 characters)
  • Meta Description — A short description under the title in search results (under 160 characters)
  • OG Image — The image shown when your page is shared on WhatsApp, LinkedIn, etc.
SEO title example

Good: "Invest in Nigeria — NIPC | IPAWAS Member"
Avoid: "Nigeria" (too short) or "Welcome to the official Nigeria Investment Promotion Commission IPAWAS page 2026" (too long).

E. Resources (Documents)

Go to Profile → Resources. Upload PDF documents that investors can download:

  • Investment Guide — Step-by-step guide for foreign investors entering your country
  • Doing Business Guide — Business registration, permits, regulations

PDFs only. Maximum file size: 50MB per file.

Understanding Opportunity Statuses

StatusMeaningVisible on public site?
Draft Created and saved, but not yet published No — only you can see it
Published Live and visible to all investors on the website Yes

Creating a New Opportunity

Click "Create Opportunity" (top-right button). The form has several sections — work through them top to bottom:

1
Basic Details

Title — Clear, specific, and investor-focused. E.g. "Solar Energy Plant — 50MW, Abuja"
Summary — 2–3 sentences. What is the opportunity and why is it attractive?
Description — Full details. Use the rich text editor to format properly.

2
Classification

Opportunity Type — e.g. Greenfield, Expansion, Joint Venture, Privatisation
Primary Sector — e.g. Agriculture, Energy, Manufacturing, ICT
Sub-sector — More specific area within the sector
Specific Location — City/region where the opportunity is located

3
Investment Details

Investment Required (min) — Minimum capital required (USD)
Investment Required (max) — Maximum capital range (USD)
Expected ROI — Estimated return on investment (e.g. "18–22%")
Payback Period — How long to recover investment (e.g. "5–7 years")
Project Stage — e.g. Concept, Feasibility, Ready to Implement

4
Market & Risk Information

Market Analysis — Who are the customers? What is the market size?
Target Market — Domestic, Regional, or International
Investor Profile — What type of investor is this suited for?
Technical Requirements — Any specific expertise or technology needed
Risk Factors — Be honest about risks
Risk Mitigation — How those risks are being addressed

5
Images

Thumbnail Image — Small image shown in listings (required for publishing)
Hero Image — Large banner image shown on the opportunity's detail page
Gallery Images — Additional photos (site photos, maps, etc.)

6
Save as Draft or Publish

Click "Save as Draft" to save without publishing, or "Publish" to make it live immediately (if you have the Publish permission).

Write for international investors, not local audiences

Investors reading your opportunities may be based in Europe, Asia, or the Americas. Avoid local acronyms, assume they don't know your country's geography, and always include currency conversions. Write in clear, professional English.

Publishing an Opportunity

Once a draft opportunity is ready to go live:

1
Find the opportunity in the list

Opportunities list → look for Draft status.

2
Click the "Publish" button

A confirmation appears. The opportunity status changes to Published and it becomes live on the website immediately.

Don't have a Publish button?

Publishing requires the "Can Publish" permission. If you don't see this button, contact your team admin or the IPAWAS HQ team to have the permission granted to your account.

Unpublishing an Opportunity

If you need to take an opportunity offline (e.g. it has been filled, or information needs updating), click "Unpublish" from the opportunity list. It returns to Draft status and is immediately hidden from the public website.

Marking an Opportunity as Featured

Featured opportunities appear prominently at the top of your country's listing page. Click the star icon (or "Feature" button) next to any published opportunity to toggle this on or off. Use this sparingly — feature only your top 1–2 opportunities.

Editing an Opportunity

Click the Edit (pencil) icon on any opportunity in the list. The same form you used to create it will open, pre-filled with existing data. Make your changes and click Save.

You can edit a published opportunity

Changes to a published opportunity take effect immediately on the public site. You do not need to unpublish first to edit it.

4. Priority Sectors

Priority sectors tell investors which industries your country is actively targeting. They appear on your country profile page. Navigate to Sidebar → Sectors.

Adding a Sector

1
Click "Add Sector"
2
Select the sector from the dropdown

e.g. Agriculture, Energy, Manufacturing, Tourism, ICT, Financial Services

3
Add a description

2–3 sentences explaining why this sector is a priority for your country.

4
Set the investment potential level

High / Medium / Low — this gives investors a quick visual indicator.

5
Tick "Priority Sector" if applicable

Priority sectors are highlighted differently on your profile page.

6
Click Save

Reordering Sectors

The order of sectors on your profile page matters — put your most attractive sectors first. On the Sectors list page, drag and drop the rows to reorder them, then click "Save Order".

5. Investor Inquiries

When an investor submits a contact form on your country's page or on a specific opportunity, it appears as an inquiry in your dashboard. Responding promptly is critical — investors who don't hear back quickly move on to other countries. Navigate to Sidebar → Inquiries.

Inquiry Statuses

StatusMeaningWhat to do
New Just received, not yet viewed Open and read it. Respond within 24–48 hours.
In Progress Being handled by your team Continue follow-up. Update status as it progresses.
Qualified Investor confirmed as serious, ready to meet Escalate to senior staff. Arrange meetings.
Closed Concluded (either successful or no longer active) Archive. No further action needed.

Opening and Responding to an Inquiry

1
Click any inquiry in the list

This opens the full inquiry detail page showing the investor's name, company, message, and contact details.

2
Read the inquiry carefully

Note what sector they're interested in, what they're asking for, and their level of seriousness.

3
Update the status

Change from "New" to "In Progress" as soon as you begin working on it. This prevents duplicate responses from your team.

4
Respond to the investor directly

Contact them using the email address they provided. The platform does not send replies on your behalf — you will email them directly from your work email.

5
Update the status as the conversation progresses

Move to "Qualified" once you've confirmed they are serious. Close the inquiry when it is concluded.

Response time is critical

International investors evaluate countries partly by how quickly they respond to initial inquiries. Aim to respond to every New inquiry within 48 hours. Delays signal disorganisation and can cost your country investment opportunities.

Assigning an Inquiry to a Team Member

On the inquiry detail page, you can assign it to a specific team member. This sends them a notification and makes them responsible for follow-up. Useful when an inquiry is in a sector that a colleague specialises in.

6. Managing Your Team

Navigate to Sidebar → Invitations to manage your team's access to the dashboard.

Who can manage the team?

You can only invite and manage team members if your account has the "Can Manage Users" permission. If you don't see these options, ask your team admin or IPAWAS HQ to grant you this permission.

Inviting a New Team Member

1
Go to Sidebar → Invitations
2
Click "Send Invitation"
3
Enter the person's email address and select their role

Roles control what they see and can do. Choose carefully:

  • Admin — Full access to everything in your dashboard
  • Editor — Can create and edit content, but cannot publish or manage users
  • Viewer — Read-only access, can view reports and inquiries
4
Add an optional message

A personal note included in the invitation email (e.g. "Welcome to the NIPC digital team!")

5
Click "Send Invitation"

The person receives an email. Once they click the link and create their account, they can access the dashboard.

What if the Invitation Expires?

Invitations expire after 7 days. If the person didn't accept in time:

  • Go to Invitations list
  • Find the expired invitation (status: Expired)
  • Click "Resend" — a new 7-day link is generated and emailed to them

Revoking an Invitation

If you invited someone by mistake or they should no longer join:

  • Find the Pending invitation in the list
  • Click "Revoke"
  • The link in their email immediately becomes invalid

9. Reaching 100% Profile Completion

A complete profile makes your country look credible and professional to investors. Here is what you need to reach 100%:

Profile Completion Checklist

  • Basic Information — Country name, IPA acronym, logo, hero image filled in
  • Economic Data — At least the core economic indicators filled in
  • Priority Sectors — At least 3 sectors added with descriptions
  • Published Opportunities — At least 1 investment opportunity published
  • Hero Image — A high-quality banner image uploaded
  • IPA Logo — Official IPA logo uploaded
Don't wait until everything is perfect

Publish what you have now, then improve over time. A 70% complete profile that is live is far better than a 100% profile that is still a draft. Investors are looking for you today.
